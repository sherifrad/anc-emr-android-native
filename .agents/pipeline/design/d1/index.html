Entry screens · two designs each

Every phone below is live: tap chips, press +/−, open rows, add and remove. All data is made-up sample data (patient Mona Hassan, dated by LMP 19 Apr 2026, so today 1 Nov 2026 is 28w 0d). Nothing here is saved anywhere. Use Text in the top bar to test large font (130% and 160%) and Mode to test light and dark.

1 · Laboratory entry

Both options: pick a panel and every test of it is added; date and lab are set once for the whole batch; the flag (Normal / High / Low) is worked out from the reference range, and you only touch it to override. Try: tap a panel you have not used yet, type a result such as 16 in the haemoglobin row, or search “ferritin”.

AOne list, type in place

  • Faster: the whole panel is on one screen; numbers are typed straight into the row; qualitative tests are two taps at most; no “Save and add another” loop.
  • Keeps: every current field: test name, category, result, unit, reference range, abnormal flag, notes, lab name, date.
  • Costs: the phone keyboard opens and closes row by row; a long panel (8 rows) needs scrolling.

BOne test at a time, number keypad

  • Faster: the keypad stays on screen, so no keyboard flicker; qualitative results move on by themselves after one tap.
  • Keeps: same fields as A; flag override and note sit under “Flag / note”; the full list is one tap away in Review.
  • Costs: you see one test at a time (Review tab shows all); typing a decimal value needs the keypad’s own dot key.

2 · Ultrasound entry

Both options use the same entry style for every finding (tap a picture card or press +/−, never a big text box). Try: raise GA weeks four times to see the dating hint; tap “From my choices” in the impression.

APicture cards and steppers

  • Faster: every choice is one tap on a picture card; every number is a stepper that starts at the typical value for the GA; the impression can be drafted from the choices.
  • Keeps: all current fields: date, GA weeks/days, linked visit, number, presentation, placenta, liquor, FHR, BPD, HC, AC, FL, EFW, photos, impression.
  • Costs: longest scroll of the two; the sections can be collapsed to a one-line summary.

BStart from a normal scan, one finding at a time

  • Faster: “Fill normal scan” answers the usual findings in one tap; after each answer the next finding opens by itself; the report line on top updates live.
  • Keeps: same fields as A; date/GA/hint in the top strip; photos and “link to visit” under More.
  • Costs: pictures are small; numbers are on a slider with +/− (finer typing is possible only by the value box); a pre-filled “normal” must be checked by the doctor before saving.

3 · Medication entry

Both options: search or pick a common/recent drug, dose / route / frequency as chips, start date defaults to today, prophylaxis toggle, notes only on request, and a list of today’s medications with quick Stop and Edit. Try: add Low-Dose Aspirin, stop Labetalol, then press Undo.

ABuild one prescription card

  • Faster: pick a drug, its usual dose, route and frequency are already selected; change only what differs, then one “Add” button.
  • Keeps: name, dose, route, frequency, start date, end date, status, prophylaxis, non-compliance, side effects, notes.
  • Costs: two taps minimum per drug (pick, Add); the card is taller than B’s one-tap add.

BOne tap to add, fix it in the list

  • Faster: one tap on a drug adds it with its usual dose and today’s date; chips on the new line change anything, with Undo.
  • Keeps: same fields as A; end date, status, non-compliance, side effects and notes sit under “⋯ More” on each line.
  • Costs: a drug can be added with the default dose by a stray tap (Undo is shown for a few seconds); no single review screen before adding.
